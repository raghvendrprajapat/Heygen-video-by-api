Create a 60-second landscape 16:9 product demo for ConvertScreen, a tool that
turns raw app screenshots into conversion-focused App Store and Google Play
screenshot sets.

FORMAT — read this first, it drives everything else.

Build this as a screen-share walkthrough, the way a developer records a Loom.
The attached screen recording is the real product and is the main subject of
the video. The presenter is the person demonstrating it, not a talking head
sitting beside unrelated footage.

- The product recording holds most of the frame for most of the runtime.
- The presenter sits in a smaller inset — a corner bubble or a side panel —
  visible throughout, so you always see who is walking you through it.
- Full-frame presenter ONLY for the opening hook and the closing line.
  Every other beat keeps the product on screen while they talk over it.
- The presenter should read as someone pointing at and reacting to what is on
  screen. They are showing you the tool.

The attachment is a silent 6 minute 32 second recording. That is intentional —
all audio comes from the narrator. Pull the moments that match each line and
cut the rest. Landmarks by approximate timestamp:

- 0:15-0:35  landing page, and a before/after showing downloads rising
- 0:45-1:05  creating a new project, choosing screenshots to upload
- 1:05-1:25  picking a style, then "Let's Create"
- 1:30-2:00  the AI assistant writing headlines; generated slides appear,

- 5:25-5:45  the export dialog asking which store you publish to

Show the recording at its native aspect ratio, pushing in slightly so UI text

TONE

This must read as a developer sharing a fix for a problem they hit, NOT as a
sponsored ad. No brand intro, no logo card, no "sponsored by", no discount
code, no "link in bio", no subscribe ask. The presenter is a peer, not a
spokesperson. Never say the words "sponsored", "partner", or "ad".

Presenter: mid-twenties to mid-thirties, plain dark solid top, neutral
background, soft key light. Upright, still, no swaying, no face or hair
touching. Direct eye contact with the lens.

Energy arc: hard and clinical on the hook, calm and instructional through the
steps, firm on the close. Tight pacing, zero filler words, hard cut at the end
with no smile and no fade.

DELIVERY DIRECTION
- Hook: no blinking, intense direct stare, staccato. Sharp downward right-hand
  chop on "screenshots".
- "THREE seconds" and "TEN store sizes": eyebrows raise, heavy vocal stress.
- Steps one/two/three: slow down, count on fingers.
- "Pretty wins compliments. Structure wins installs.": precision pinch
  gesture, falling pitch.
- Close: lean slightly toward the lens, furrowed brows, stern. Hard cut.

CAPTIONS
Burn in captions, bottom-centre, bold sans-serif, high contrast, a few words at
a time. Emphasise the numbers.

Follow the script below exactly, scene by scene. Do not add lines, do not
rewrite lines, and do not append a call to action.

SCRIPT

Your app isn't losing installs because of the app. It's the screenshots.

Most developers upload raw simulator screens. Nobody taps those.

A visitor judges your listing in THREE seconds.

The old fix? A designer. A week of waiting. A few hundred dollars. Then you redo all of it for TEN store sizes.

Here is how it works.

Step one. Drop in your raw app screens. Nothing to install.

Step two. The AI writes the headlines and reorders your screens into an actual conversion story. Hook. Feature. Proof. Close. Every slide gets a job.

Step three. Export one ZIP. Every store size, named and ready to upload.

And here is the part most people don't realize. Most screenshot tools stop at pretty. Pretty wins compliments. Structure wins installs.

Your screenshots are not decoration. They are your conversion rate.

So here is the real takeaway. Before you ship another feature, go look at your store listing. Fix that first.
